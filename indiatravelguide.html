Documents Checklist
Everything we carried — passport, e-visa, and the two documents we kept as backup even though nobody asked for them.

What we carried

  • Baby's Canadian passport
  • Baby's e-visa / OCI, as applicable
  • Birth certificate — original and copies
  • Vaccination record
  • Signed consent letter — recommended even if not always asked for

What actually happened at the airport

At the family assistance line, we filled out a self-declaration health form (tied to current outbreak monitoring — always check what's required before you fly, this changes). They asked for baby's passport and e-visa, and confirmed the health form. Birth registration and consent letter weren't asked for — but I was glad to have them just in case.

The 2-Diaper-Bag Packing System
Pack two fully self-sufficient bags, not one — swap at the halfway mark instead of digging through one overstuffed bag mid-flight.
LiveTap to read ↓

The system

Pack 2 fully self-sufficient diaper bags — each one with a complete set of everything you need (diapers, wipes, a spare outfit, snacks, a toy). Not split by category, split by time.

Why this works

Use Bag 1 for the first half of the flight. Once it's used up, switch to Bag 2 for the second half. You're never digging through one overstuffed bag trying to find one thing — everything you need for that stretch is already together.

What to include in each bag

  • Diapers + wipes
  • A spare outfit
  • Snacks
  • A toy or two
  • Anything else your baby might need for a few hours, self-contained

Take a Stroller, Not Just a Carrier
What happened when our bag handle broke mid-security — and why I won't fly without a stroller as backup again.
LiveTap to read ↓

I didn't bring a stroller for our 17-hour flight — I had a carrier and figured that was enough. Then at airport security, the handle on my check-in bag (the one I was using to carry my diaper bag) broke. Suddenly I had baby in the carrier, one diaper bag as a backpack, another in my hand, and no working handle to attach anything to. My mother-in-law was travelling with us and grabbed the cabin bag, or I genuinely don't know how I'd have managed alone.

What I'd tell you

Bring a stroller, even if you plan to wear your baby most of the time. Most airlines let you check it right at the gate, not at check-in — so it doesn't count as extra luggage. A carrier is great, but it's not a backup plan if something goes wrong with your bags. And with a baby, something always can.

The Form Nobody Tells You to Fill Out in Advance
The one that ate into our time at Delhi immigration — and how to have it done before you land.
LiveTap to read ↓

At the immigration counter, I had to fill out an e-Arrival form on the spot. It kept giving me trouble — whether it was the connection, the form itself, or just doing it exhausted after a 17-hour flight with a baby, it ate up time I didn't have, standing right there at the counter with people waiting behind us.

What I'd tell you

Fill this out in advance, online, before you even get to the airport. Don't wait until you're standing at immigration, jetlagged, holding a baby, trying to troubleshoot a form on your phone. One less thing to deal with when you land.

Where to fill it out

Official e-Arrival Card portal: indianvisaonline.gov.in/earrival (submit up to 72 hours before arrival)

All passengers also need the Air Suvidha Self-Declaration Form: airsuvidha.civilaviation.gov.in

Both are completely free — beware of third-party sites charging a fee for "assistance."

Fermented Ragi Idli (Flight-Ready)
Soft, familiar, and easy for baby to eat with her hands mid-flight.
LiveTap to read ↓

Ingredients

  • 2 spoons fermented ragi powder
  • 2 spoons sooji (semolina)
  • 2 spoons poha, soaked 5 min & ground
  • 1 spoon any baby masala
  • 2 spoons curd (plus 2 more later)
  • Water, as needed
  • Ghee, for coating the idli maker

Method

  • Mix ragi powder, sooji, ground poha, baby masala, and 2 spoons curd well.
  • Let the batter rest for 10 minutes.
  • Add 2 more spoons curd and enough water for a smooth, pourable consistency.
  • Coat the idli maker with ghee.
  • Add batter into the moulds.
  • Steam for 15 minutes.
  • Let rest 2–3 minutes before removing.

Travel tip

Pack in silver foil, then a zip lock bag — light and mess-free for a flight bag.

Travel Khichdi — Cooked & Packed for a Long-Haul Flight
Simple, familiar, and easy to portion for the flight.

Alongside the ragi idli, I also cooked and packed khichdi for the flight — simple, familiar, and easy to portion. Both travelled well, and Pookie ate happily through the flight without anything new or unpredictable.

My rule for flying with a baby

Never introduce a new food on travel day. Stick to what they already know and love — the flight is stressful enough without adding a first-time reaction to the mix.

✓
Vegetable Daliya / Upma (Flight-Ready)
A savoury, veggie-packed option that travels well in a milk cooler.
LiveTap to read ↓

Ingredients

  • 1 spoon ghee (clarified butter)
  • Cumin
  • Tomato
  • Broccoli, carrots, peas, and zucchini
  • Spices of your taste, except salt and chilli
  • 2 spoons daliya or upma
  • Water, as needed

Method

  • Add ghee, then cumin and tomato.
  • Add broccoli, carrots, peas, and zucchini.
  • Add your spices, except salt and chilli.
  • Add the daliya or upma.
  • Pressure cook for 2–3 whistles.
  • Open and mash the veggies, adding water to reach the consistency you need.
  • Let it cool completely.

Travel tip

Pack it in one bottle inside a portable breast milk cooler, along with 2 yogurt cups in the same cooler. To serve on the flight, mix in a little hot water first.

Bottles, Formula & Water — Our Real Numbers
Exactly how I packed formula for a 17-hour flight — no digging, no guessing mid-air.

  • Bottles total: 9
  • Pre-filled with water: 3
  • Empty, filled with hot water on the plane: 6
  • Pre-measured milk powder portions: 8 — one per feeding, no scooping mid-flight

Why it worked

No digging through bags mid-flight. No guessing portions while exhausted. Everything pre-measured and ready to go.
